About me

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at Purdue University, advised by Prof. Yuxiang Peng. My research interests include language models and natural language processing, information retrieval, AI for mathematics, and AI evaluation.

I am broadly interested in understanding and improving the capabilities, efficiency, and reliability of modern AI systems. My work explores how these systems can better retrieve information, reason about complex problems, and be evaluated for real-world use.

Previously, I completed my M.S. in Computer Science and Engineering at The Ohio State University, where I worked with Prof. Sachin Kumar. I also conducted research at Academia Sinica with Prof. Chuan-Ju Wang and received my undergraduate degrees from National Chengchi University.

When Cases Get Rare: A Retrieval Benchmark for Off-Guideline Clinical Question Answering

Doeun Lee, Muge Zhang, Yi Yu, Ashish Manne, Stephen Koesters, Frank Wen, Brady Buchanan, Lynda Villagomez, Oluwatoba Moninuola, James Lim, Kathryn Tobin, Andrew Srisuwananukorn, Ping Zhang, and Sachin Kumar

View details

Abstract

Clinical guidelines cannot cover the long tail of real-world medical cases, while existing medical language-model evaluations often emphasize common knowledge and multiple-choice questions. We introduce OGCaReBench, an expert-validated benchmark of long-form, off-guideline clinical questions derived from medical case reports. Experiments show that current models struggle on these questions, while retrieval augmentation substantially improves performance and underscores the importance of evidence-grounded clinical reasoning.

MMLF: Multi-query Multi-passage Late Fusion Retrieval

Yuan-Ching Kuo, Yi Yu, Chih-Ming Chen, and Chuan-Ju Wang

View details

Abstract

Large language models are effective for query expansion, but prior work has focused more on query formatting and prompting than on handling retrieval results. MMLF generates sub-queries, expands them into pseudo-documents, retrieves each independently, and aggregates the results using reciprocal rank fusion. Across five BEIR datasets, this simple pipeline improves both Recall@1k and nDCG@10 over strong baselines.
